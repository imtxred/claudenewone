„When you can no longer stand on your own, little by little you become a burden to your own family."

A young Nigerian doctor who goes to the forgotten elderly of Enugu and Ebonyi — and gives them back what no one else can: the ability to walk, their freedom, and their place in the family.

Published: , 09:36 · By: our editorial writer

We arrived on a Tuesday morning in a small village in Enugu State. The dirt road runs past old fences and barking dogs. „Are you looking for the young doctor?" — a woman in front of a small shop asked us. „He comes every Thursday. The old people wait for him at the gate. Some of them cry when they see him."

That was how we first heard about Dr. Emeka Okafor — the 31-year-old doctor who left his good, well-paid job at a private hospital in Lagos, to do what no other doctor wanted to do: to travel around the villages of south-eastern Nigeria, and treat the elderly whom, little by little, even their own families had begun to abandon.

The reason is almost always the same: a person suddenly can no longer stand. Their knee refuses to work. Their back bends. Their hand trembles so much they cannot even hold a spoon. And exactly at that moment — when they need help the most — they become the very thing they feared their whole life: a burden.

„My son told me: «Mama, I can't keep lifting you out of the chair every time. It's no longer possible.» I'm not angry with him. He works, he has a family. But since then he rarely comes. And I just sit, waiting for someone to remember me." — Mama Adaeze, 79, Nsukka
„All my life I was the one who took the grandchildren to school and brought them back, who cooked, who was there in times of trouble. When my knees stopped working, suddenly I became the one who needed to be cared for. And I saw the tiredness in my daughter's eyes. That is what hurts the most — not the knee. It's that I have become a burden." — Papa Sunday, 73, Abakaliki

„I saw what it means to be unwanted. And I decided I could not just watch"

An exclusive interview with Dr. Emeka Okafor — the doctor who gave up a high salary to help those who no longer have anyone.

Dr. Emeka Okafor: „The first time I entered the home of an old woman as a volunteer, I was 28. I was not ready for what I saw. A 76-year-old woman sat on the edge of the bed, her legs swollen, her knees that could no longer bend. For four months she had not left her room. She took my hand and said: «Doctor, my children left me when I could no longer climb the stairs.» At that moment I understood: these people need me the most."

Dr. Emeka Okafor speaks calmly, but there is a tension in his voice that you cannot ignore. For two years he has been travelling around the villages of south-eastern Nigeria — from Enugu to Ebonyi — and everywhere he meets the same picture: elderly people with destroyed joints who receive no real treatment, given only painkillers that ruin their stomach and kidneys.

„In Nigeria, a grandmother or grandfather is often the main pillar of the family. She is the one who takes the child to school and brings them home, she is the one who cooks, she is the one who looks after the grandchildren while her daughter or son goes to work. When an elderly person can no longer walk because of their joints, they don't just lose the ability to walk. They lose their place in the family. They can no longer help, they can no longer be useful. This is the word I hear again and again from my patients: «burden». They call their own parents a burden."

„They didn't send me away because they don't love me. But because at three in the morning I would wake up screaming from the pain in my knee, and no one could sleep. My daughter-in-law said: either her, or me. My son chose her." — Papa Musa, 71, Awgu

„Pills don't cure anything — they only cover up the damage"

Dr. Emeka Okafor explains why conventional treatment fails.

Dr. Emeka Okafor: „Let me be honest: what I saw in these homes frightened me. People of 55–80 years, who for years have been given the same painkillers — drugs that silence the pain for a few hours, but do not treat the cause. Their cartilage keeps breaking down. The joint fluid dries up. The joint wears down to the bone. And the chemicals in those pills, day after day, ruin their stomach, liver and kidneys."

It is a vicious circle: pain → a pill → the pain returns stronger → a stronger pill → the stomach refuses → stomach drugs follow → the kidneys refuse. And no one tells them the truth: that there is a way to repair the joint from the inside, not just to reduce the pain.

Dr. Emeka Okafor: „For many elderly people, the cartilage in the knee, spine or hip is destroyed by 70–90%. If they still walk, they walk bone on bone. Every step is torture. Ordinary pills cannot rebuild cartilage — they only cover up the pain while everything falls apart."

  • A feeling of a „knife" in the lower back when you straighten up;

  • Night pain that keeps you from sleeping — waking 4–5 times a night;
